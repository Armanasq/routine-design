# Adaptive Onboarding and Earning Autonomy

## Central principle

The user should not have to configure their entire life to obtain help. Routin learns progressively from authorized integrations and explicit corrections, while the user's goals and action permissions remain user-owned.

Onboarding is an evolving **evidence-quality and delegation agreement** rather than a one-time survey of preferences and goals.

## From connection to personal model

### Phase 0 — Minimal capability discovery
The user specifies a small initial goal and chooses whether to connect any sources. Routin must be useful for one-off text tasks even without an integration. It describes what it can and cannot do with available evidence, and does not invent personalized context.

### Phase 1 — Scoped source observation
Each provider adapter declares exact read scopes and coverage. Imported events are typed and deduplicated. Routin distinguishes tentative commitments, externally confirmed meetings and inferred routines, avoiding premature "you always do X" claims.

### Phase 2 — Value-sensitive questioning
Ask only about distinctions that change a meaningful next action: e.g., whether protected evenings are a hard constraint, whether the current work belongs to study/skill development or delegated execution, and whether a potential action may affect third parties. A new preference is scoped to the smallest supported context rather than globalized.

### Phase 3 — Shadow assistance
Routin silently prepares drafts, identifies missing dependencies, forecasts clashes and proposes limited alternatives. User edits become evidence of a local correction—not permission or truth of a global preference.

### Phase 4 — Bounded operational autonomy
Explicit user grants can authorize a narrow class of reversible actions (e.g., changing internal plan blocks). Actual dispatch and external writes require validated source, recipient and current grants. Permissions are not expanded because a model predicted comfort with autonomy.

### Phase 5 — Longitudinal calibrated assistance
Aggregate outcomes and corrections over time, maintain current-scoped beliefs, adjust duration forecasts and interruption policies, and identify reusable procedures with failure evidence. Avoid constant querying and learn when to leave the user alone.

## Learning preference from integration is a scientific problem

Calendar data can reveal frequent patterns, but cannot reveal whether a user *wanted* those patterns. A gym log may show activity but not medical recovery. A reading log may show access but not comprehension. An email thread may show user responsiveness but not that replies should be automated.

Use hypotheses with explicit provenance, temporal scope and counterevidence. When user values and behavioral patterns disagree, preserve explicitly chosen goals and ask only when a real decision cannot be made safely.

## Autonomy ledger

Record the exact grant, scope, recipient/tool, expiration, approved action class, observed behavior corrections and rationale. Proposed procedural skills and domain packs do not carry permissions when installed or learned. Users can revoke scopes without the system treating the act as mere preference feedback.

## Receptivity and interruption budgets

Use available context signals conservatively. Do not infer high-confidence emotional or health state from ambient telemetry. If safe and useful, propose a preference about notifications (e.g. "avoid messages during meetings"); don't infer one from a single dismiss. A quiet user may simply be unavailable rather than satisfied.

## Cross-domain shared context by minimal disclosure

An internal time planner may need to know 17:00–19:00 is unavailable, but a workplace email writer need not know it is a medical appointment, gym session or family event. Store raw protected evidence within the proper domain and share only a policy-approved derived availability constraint across the planner.

## Progress versus dependence

Routin should learn to do routine administrative work without growing an unhealthy dependence on it for user-chosen skills. A learning goal may justify preserving practice time and reducing hints as competence improves; a financial administrative request may justify minimizing explanations while still preserving approval checkpoints.

## Validation

Simulate new users with no integrations, a single connected source, contradictory old data, unreliable coverage, sensitive-domain revocation and later changed preferences. Measure first useful outcome without extensive setup, high-impact incorrect inferences, unnecessary question count, correction time and ability to revoke/delete. No improvement is claimed until tested.
