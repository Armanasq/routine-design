# Partial Intentions and Situated Plans

**Research sources:** Bratman (1987), Suchman (1987), Kruglanski et al. (2002), Wrosch et al. (2003), Herroelen and Leus (2004).

## Synthesis

Bratman treats intentions as partial plans that organize future conduct across time and people. Suchman cautions that plans do not fully determine situated action. Goal-systems research distinguishes an end from the several means that can achieve it. Robust scheduling research helps preserve accepted temporal commitments amid change.

These are compatible only if Routin maintains a hierarchy of *revisability*, not one monolithic task priority.

## Five levels of commitment

1. **User-chosen ends:** desired goal, reason optional, reconsideration authority always with user.
2. **Social commitments:** explicitly accepted obligations involving other people, requiring coordination before revision.
3. **Protected constraints:** user-stated limits, exceptions only with valid user confirmation.
4. **Current means:** scheduling methods, activity selection and workflow sequencing; usually revisable.
5. **Operational steps:** tool calls and preparatory tasks, revisable only before irreversible effects.

Routin may freely optimize lower-level means within its grants, but it must not revise the ends to justify a locally convenient plan. Conversely, the system must allow the user to change or retire goals without guilt or artificial streak penalties.

## Three distinct replan questions

- **What became infeasible?** Estimate uncertainty, time, dependencies and source reliability.
- **What can change without changing intent?** Explore alternative times, scope or means.
- **Whose agreement is needed?** A new colleague schedule is not the assistant's unilateral decision.

A surprise event may justify a local repair rather than full calendar reoptimization. Apply hysteresis, frozen windows and weighted churn to previously accepted commitments.

## Counterexample

A user repeatedly skips a planned evening robotics session but still wants to learn. The correct interpretation could be an infeasible *means*, not an abandoned *goal*. The agent should test an alternative learning modality or time before proposing to downgrade the goal.

## Experimental test

Compare rigid planning, unconstrained reactive replanning, and intention-preserving local revision. Measure missed accepted obligations, plan churn, false goal abandonment, user correction effort, and personal autonomy. No claim that a particular numeric weight is optimal before validation.
