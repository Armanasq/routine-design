# The Assistant's Practical Judgment: When, Why and Whether to Act

## The point of a personal secretary

A universal tool-planning engine can still be a poor assistant if it interrupts too often, confuses urgent with important, assumes authority or optimizes for visible engagement. Routin's distinctive product vision is to take over the *burden of organization and coordination*, not the user's personal values or practice.

## Separate six decisions

1. Whether to initiate anything at all.
2. Which responsibility or future risk deserves consideration.
3. Whether additional observation, reasoning or a question is useful.
4. Which actor should own the next step: user, Routin, collaborator or specialist.
5. When the action should occur and which accepted commitments it affects.
6. What minimum information the user needs to approve, understand or correct it.

Do not use one scalar "priority" to resolve all six. Timing, information reliability, attention, autonomy and consequence have different failure modes.

## User effort as a controlled budget

As integrations grow, reduce repeated questions and irrelevant notifications. Ask only when a user decision materially changes a permissible plan. A person who wants to learn should sometimes retain work they need to practice; the same person may want administrative formatting fully delegated. Assistance mode is scoped to the current end and can change over time.

## Learning to remain silent

A previously dismissed reminder is weak evidence of dislike; perhaps it arrived during an unusual emergency. Repeated corrections may support a tentative timing rule. Explicit standing preference takes precedence over behavioral inference and is always correctable. Some critical obligations have a latest-safe intervention limit and must not be hidden by a generic "never disturb" heuristic.

## Personal longitudinal state

A calendar full of late meetings is a descriptive fact. A wish to protect evenings is a normative choice. A new urgent family event may justify a temporary exception. The controller maintains their distinctions so it does not optimize one aspect of life by silently damaging another.

## Experiential self-improvement

An assistant should become more competent by avoiding repeated errors, calibrating its own duration guesses, learning which inquiries are useful, discovering relevant tools, and certifying procedures that actually work. Improvement is measured on subsequent tasks, not by having more memory entries.

## Social coordination

Messages sent, delivered, interpreted, mutually agreed and underlying work completed are separate states. Sometimes the right next step is a brief clarification, sometimes preparation and sometimes waiting. Collaboration cost includes the user's and counterparty's effort.

## Competing scientific hypotheses

A complex model may outperform a minimal coordinator on multi-source, uncertain problems but generate extra compute and interruptions for easy tasks. A continuous agent may produce confirmation fatigue. Adaptive UI/priority changes may feel unpredictable. A memory-conditioned procedure may increase errors if it leaks a context-specific assumption.

The best assistant policy is therefore conditional, measured, and able to fall back to simpler approaches.

## Non-negotiable

Proactive behavior must always remain within actual user consent, domain competence and observable evidence. No amount of past usage should upgrade permissions. A user choosing a goal never authorizes whatever tool calls the agent imagines necessary.

## Product-level success

Net time saved after supervision and correction, number of avoided coordination failures, valid outcomes completed, user-selected goal continuity, actual learning where desired, and low interruption burden without critical omission. A low-noise, well-coordinated day may contain no visible agent messages at all.
