## Established prior art
Horvitz's LookOut-related principles already describe inference of user goals under uncertainty, intervention timing, cost-benefit tradeoffs and collaboration between automation and user control. This paper substantially limits novelty claims for "AI secretary asks/acts at the right time" without measurable methodological advances.

## Design derivation
The selection policy should first determine whether the action belongs to the user or the system, then whether preparation can proceed silently, then the latest safe time to surface unresolved risk. The best policy may ask, act within a grant, prepare, monitor or abstain. The availability of an LLM is not sufficient justification to make it the decision authority.

## Interactions
Grounding theory (P037) adds communication-completion costs; contextual preference research (P041) tests whether the chosen initiative actually follows the user's interaction preferences; transactional workflow research (P027) determines what can be delegated reliably.

## Limitation and experiment
The 1999 work is foundational design theory with a system illustration; it does not define universal modern mobile-agent error rates. Compare deterministic baseline, ask-all, act-all and constrained initiative with measured user corrections, missed critical events, unauthorized effects and counterparty friction.
