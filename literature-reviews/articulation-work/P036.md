# P036 — Taking CSCW Seriously: Supporting Articulation Work

**Authors:** Kjeld Schmidt; Liam J. Bannon. **Year:** 1992. **Venue:** Computer Supported Cooperative Work.

## Why it matters
The paper argues that cooperative work depends on articulation: aligning the tasks, resources, actors and timing that allow distributed work to proceed. This differs from providing communication tools or managing one person's task queue. It is a conceptual CSCW contribution, not a personal-agent intervention trial.

## Derivation for Routin
Treat each handoff as a relation with owner, counterpart, expected outcome, expectation status, required preparation, earliest/latest useful coordination and evidence coverage. Coordination itself consumes time and should appear in realistic capacity budgets. A third party may not have agreed to a proposal merely because it was sent.

## Counterexample
A collaborator can complete work offline while an email inbox has no reply. Absent communication is not proof of absent progress. First establish whether a response was expected, which source has coverage, and whether the source is fresh.

## Cross-paper consequence
Combined with Clark and Brennan (P037), the handoff needs a threshold for mutual understanding. Combined with sagas (P027), it needs durable state and recovery. Combined with secretarial ethnography (P001), it should trigger anticipatory preparation, not premature nagging.

## Discriminating test
Task extraction versus coordination-episode tracking: expected handoff recall, premature follow-ups, false commitments, time to actual mutual agreement, and counterpart effort. A larger number of messages is not success.
