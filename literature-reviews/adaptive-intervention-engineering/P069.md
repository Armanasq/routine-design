# P069 — An expandable approach for design and personalization of digital, just-in-time adaptive interventions

**Authors:** Suat Gonul, Tuncay Namli, Sasja Huisman, Gokce Banu Laleci Erturkmen, Ismail Hakki Toroslu and Ahmet Cosar  
**Publication:** 2019, Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association 26(3), 198–210  
**Primary source:** https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocy160  
**Review evidence:** `open_access_abstract_methods_simulation_results_summary`, verified on 2026-11-16. This is an original critical synthesis; methods/results are inspected only to the degree indicated by the access label.

## 1. Question and investigated methods
Proposes a JITAI template architecture and rule definition language for intervention triggers, incorporating momentary/historical context. Evaluates example definitions, diabetes-care program rules and a simulation with two personas for personalization decisions.

## 2. Empirical result or theoretical contribution
Demonstrates feasibility of a configurable rule-driven intervention design and simulated adaptation. Shows explicit decision points and tailoring rules can be represented as reusable machine-processable constructs.

## 3. Boundaries, assumptions, negative evidence
Two simulated personas do not establish longitudinal health benefit or safe autonomy. This is substantial prior art for domain-pack DSL design and personalized intervention timing; Routin cannot claim generic configurability is new.

## 4. Concrete Routin policy or architectural implication
Represent packs as typed schema+rule+evidence+grant+verifier bundles. Use hard-coded safety and signed pack versions for consequential behavior, not unrestricted runtime-generated intervention scripts.

## 5. Cross-disciplinary mechanism synthesis
Combine foundational JITAI P061 with agency framework P060 and evidence/permission semantics from Routin. Unlike a health-only intervention framework, test cross-domain composition and negative transfer explicitly.

## 6. Falsifiable test and acceptance
Check same pack under changed goal, missing source, revoked grant, time budgeting conflict and high-risk request. Compare correctly scoped intervention vs indiscriminate template use; quantify unsafe behavior and intervention fatigue.
