## Formal problem
The paper provides a foundational logical account of belief contraction and revision when new information conflicts with an existing body of beliefs. This is a formal normative treatment of propositions, not an empirical investigation of noisy multimodal personal-agent data.

## Routin interpretation
Preferences, identities and deadlines cannot be updated by blindly appending the newest message. A conflicting assertion may justify suspending a claim, narrowing it to a context, revising its validity interval, or requesting clarification. A mere rumor about deadline extension cannot overwrite the confirmed deadline.

## Safe approximation
Implement a provenance-backed temporal belief ledger rather than assuming the strong logical closure of classical AGM. Store competing claims and invalidate only what is logically/evidentially unsupported; keep explicit user instructions distinct from inferred descriptive beliefs. Propagate source deletion and revoked access to dependent cached beliefs.

## Connections and limitations
Memora (P023) exposes the cost of remembering invalid information, while P040 warns against mistaking raw data accumulation for knowledge. For uncertain language, exact logical contraction is only a conceptual guide.

## Experiment
Contradictory same-day messages, one-week exceptions, superseded preferences, stale sources and partial third-party reports. Evaluate current-state decisions and erroneous revisions against last-write-wins and append-only memory.
