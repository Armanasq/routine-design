# S17 — When Automation Harms the Goal of Learning

**Sources:** P063 Dunlosky, P064 Cepeda, P065 Zimmerman, P067 Kestin et al. (2025 positive randomized tutor study), P068 Liu et al. (2026 negative randomized tutor-access study), and P024 deskilling.

## The apparent conflict

The 2025 Scientific Reports crossover study reported stronger short-term learning under a structured AI tutor than its comparison lesson in a physics setting. The October 2026 working-paper RCT across 2,379 students reported decreased grades and LMS participation after providing access to a course-integrated tutor.

These studies do not test identical tutoring treatments, environments, participant populations, scaffolding, or outcomes. It is improper to average the effects as if they were identical, claim the second falsifies the first, or assume a generic AI tutor always helps.

Dunlosky/Cepeda support testing/spacing methods; Zimmerman describes planning, self-monitoring and reflection. Their connection suggests that **the user must demonstrate learning**, rather than letting an assistant substitute for the learning activities.

## Derived assistance modes

*Delegate* requests may legitimately be completed by Routin. *Learn/practice* requests should preserve user-controlled problem solving, retrieval and feedback. A tutor may explain and scaffold but should not automatically solve graded work or claim mastery after a fluent conversation.

Tutoring is not merely changing response style: it changes the outcome contract, what is measured, the appropriate intervention dose, and which actions would be counterproductive.

## Counterexample

User asks Routin to draft a job application letter: delegation is appropriate when authorized. The same user asks to learn persuasive writing: drafting everything without practice may undermine skill development. A globally applied "autonomous writing" preference would confuse these goals.

## Experiment

Randomize task-level support modes with stated user objectives, compare equal learning time and model cost, include delayed unassisted tests and objective task quality, report subgroup/adverse effects, and retain a normal non-tutor fallback. Do not ship a tutoring policy based only on retention of a conversation or app engagement.
