# S18 — The Right Intervention Is Sometimes No Intervention

**Sources:** P061 JITAI, P062 COM-B, P070 dietary systematic review, P006 notification timing, P008 value-of-information, P066 GPTCoach and P060 longitudinal agency.

## Cross-disciplinary connection

Administrative assistants protect attention; behavior-change specialists tailor interventions to opportunity and receptivity. JITAI formalizes proximal/distal outcomes, decision points, tailoring variables, intervention options and rules. Combined, this points toward a policy that decides not only *what deserves attention* but whether a particular form of intervention would create useful change *at this moment*.

A 2026 dietary JITAI systematic review of 20 studies/2,948 participants found more consistent proximal behavioral changes than distal clinical outcomes. GPTCoach's 2025 small supervised single-session study did not establish sustained behavior change. Therefore optimizing prompt responses or exercise adherence as if they prove health improvement is unsound.

## Combined algorithm (hypothesis)

Candidate intervention = (goal, domain pack, consequence, last useful time, user receptivity evidence, available agency mode, source reliability, question cost, side effects, targeted proximal outcome, targeted distal outcome).

First enforce authority/health boundary, then evaluate if any intervention can plausibly improve an outcome at acceptable burden, including explicit NOOP and resync. If user's current situation or goal is uncertain and the question itself is costly, use selective clarification. Critical verified deadlines can override ordinary batching; unrelated prompts should not.

## Negative effects to test

Excessive reminders, inferred motivation from hidden state, one week of unusual behavior becoming a standing goal, a helpful workout cue arriving during a critical work emergency, and behavioral compliance improving while user-chosen wellbeing worsens.

## Experimental protocol

Compare no interventions, fixed timing, generic urgency rules and context/risk-gated options. Separate proximal participation, distal outcomes, intervention fatigue, user autonomy and hard safety events; never combine into a flattering single engagement metric.
