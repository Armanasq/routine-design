# P070 — Application of Just-in-Time Adaptive Interventions in Dietary Health Management: Systematic Review

**Authors:** Chang Ying Li, Zhen Zhu Jiao, Li Qin Zhang, Hang Li, Meng Yao Wang, Wen Hui Guo, Yu Xin Wang and Yang Wang  
**Publication:** 2026, Journal of Medical Internet Research 28, e92139  
**Primary source:** https://doi.org/10.2196/92139  
**Review evidence:** `open_access_registered_systematic_review_abstract_results_selection_and_conclusions`, verified on 2026-11-16. This is an original critical synthesis; methods/results are inspected only to the degree indicated by the access label.

## 1. Question and investigated methods
PROSPERO-registered review (CRD420261285292) identified 20 included studies involving 2,948 participants on dietary JITAI; methods describe database/registry search and quality assessment.

## 2. Empirical result or theoretical contribution
More consistent improvements in proximal behaviors and process outcomes than in distal clinical measures. Some studies report weight/BMI, blood pressure or glucose changes, but findings are inconsistent and most did not demonstrate clear between-group advantages.

## 3. Boundaries, assumptions, negative evidence
Population, content, outcome and intervention heterogeneity limit any single effect claim. Associations with proximal metrics do not establish long-term clinical benefits; behavior feedback can be burdensome or inappropriate in certain conditions.

## 4. Concrete Routin policy or architectural implication
Routin's intervention contract must explicitly separate immediate adherence/engagement from downstream health or wellbeing results; never infer clinical improvement from a successfully delivered suggestion.

## 5. Cross-disciplinary mechanism synthesis
Couple P061 JITAI policy with P059 specialist health architecture, P060 longitudinal agency and COM-B P062 opportunity/motivation distinction. Human constraints and chosen goals determine whether a prompt should be offered.

## 6. Falsifiable test and acceptance
Assess proximal behavior, longer-term objective, number of unwanted nudges, reversibility, health safety and reported burden separately. Compare a no-prompt baseline and audit adverse or null intervention effects.
