# P064 — Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis

**Authors:** Nicholas J. Cepeda, Harold Pashler, Edward Vul, John T. Wixted and Doug Rohrer  
**Publication:** 2006, Psychological Bulletin 132(3), 354–380  
**Primary source:** https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354  
**Review evidence:** `pubmed_abstract_and_original_meta_analysis_summary`, verified on 2026-11-16. This is an original critical synthesis; methods/results are inspected only to the degree indicated by the access label.

## 1. Question and investigated methods
Quantitative synthesis covers 839 assessments drawn from 317 experiments in 184 articles, analyzing spacing and retention intervals in verbal-recall tasks.

## 2. Empirical result or theoretical contribution
Spacing effects depend on retention interval; longer desired retention changes which interstudy intervals are useful. This supports scheduling deliberate spaced retrieval rather than maximizing one-day study length.

## 3. Boundaries, assumptions, negative evidence
The outcome is verbal recall, not guaranteed transfer to motor skills, deep research competence or clinical behavior. Exact optimal intervals depend on content and learner and are not fixed at one universal cadence.

## 4. Concrete Routin policy or architectural implication
Implement separate prospective recall opportunities for learning goals with time-budget and user-consent checks; allow deviations without shaming or wiping mastery state.

## 5. Cross-disciplinary mechanism synthesis
Combine with P063 active testing and P065 self-regulated learning. Do not treat gym recovery spacing as equivalent to cognitive spaced repetition.

## 6. Falsifiable test and acceptance
Compare fixed daily prompts, interval heuristics, and evidence-calibrated scheduling for delayed independent recall while measuring intervention overload and interference with other commitments.
