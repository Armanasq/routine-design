# P063 — Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology

**Authors:** John Dunlosky, Katherine A. Rawson, Elizabeth J. Marsh, Mitchell J. Nathan and Daniel T. Willingham  
**Publication:** 2013, Psychological Science in the Public Interest 14(1), 4–58  
**Primary source:** https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266  
**Review evidence:** `publisher_abstract_and_formal_results_overview`, verified on 2026-11-16. This is an original critical synthesis; methods/results are inspected only to the degree indicated by the access label.

## 1. Question and investigated methods
Critical review of ten widely used learning techniques, evaluating their generality across learning conditions, student characteristics, materials and outcome tests.

## 2. Empirical result or theoretical contribution
Practice testing and distributed practice received high utility assessments across many contexts; highlighting/re-reading do not consistently create similar gains. A learner's subjective fluency or time spent is not an objective mastery measure.

## 3. Boundaries, assumptions, negative evidence
Utility categories do not settle all disciplines or learner abilities; outcome must reflect the type of knowledge and practical performance. Not a study of an LLM tutor or long-horizon automated secretary.

## 4. Concrete Routin policy or architectural implication
Routin's learning pack must distinguish content exposure from retrieval ability, transfer and delayed retention, respect user choice and coordinate practice with realistic time budgets. Default to useful practice in learn-mode, not automatic answers.

## 5. Cross-disciplinary mechanism synthesis
Connect P064 spacing intervals and P065 forethought/performance/reflection; compare P067 positive AI tutoring RCT and P068 negative RCT to avoid a universal AI-tutoring-benefit claim.

## 6. Falsifiable test and acceptance
Compare summary-only assistance, question-generation/active practice and independent delayed assessments, measuring knowledge retention, learner agency and workload displacement, not click counts.
