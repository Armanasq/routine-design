# P067 — AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning: an RCT introducing a novel research-based design in an authentic educational setting

**Authors:** Greg Kestin, Kelly Miller, Anna Klales, Timothy Milbourne and Gregorio Ponti  
**Publication:** 2025, Scientific Reports 15, 17458  
**Primary source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6  
**Review evidence:** `open_access_article_methods_results_discussion_and_limitations_selected`, verified on 2026-11-16. This is an original critical synthesis; methods/results are inspected only to the degree indicated by the access label.

## 1. Question and investigated methods
Randomized crossover design in introductory university physics content comparing a carefully scaffolded AI tutor used at home against classroom active-learning lessons. Uses pre/post assessments of surface tension and fluid flow; article reports aggregate groups with AI and in-class postscore distributions.

## 2. Empirical result or theoretical contribution
In its assessed setting, tutor group post-test outcomes and learner experience improved, with many AI participants spending less than 60 minutes on material. Pedagogy was deliberately structured, teacher-authored and aligned to learning objectives.

## 3. Boundaries, assumptions, negative evidence
Small specific learning units, selected student population, tightly designed instructor prompts and media; not evidence that generic unguided chatbot use, arbitrary learning domains or fully autonomous AI teaching is superior.

## 4. Concrete Routin policy or architectural implication
Routin should provide structured, source-grounded, practice-oriented tutoring when user chooses learning, and evaluate objective mastery. The correct design may preserve independent exercise and support later transfer rather than simply produce fluent explanations.

## 5. Cross-disciplinary mechanism synthesis
Contrast with P068 negative multi-course tutoring trial. Combined evidence indicates tutor deployment/context and displaced participation can change effects; do not generalize positive score to all learning.

## 6. Falsifiable test and acceptance
Run matched longitudinal tests comparing scaffolding, unguided access and independent study; measure retention, transfer, course participation, subgroup differences and cognitive offloading.
