# P076: Continual Learning Exam: Benchmarking Long-Horizon RSI and Experiential Learning in LLM Agents

**Authors:** Haoyu Zhao, Sitian Tu, Yuxuan Huang, Huichi Zhou, Zhangyue Yin and coauthors  
**Year/venue:** 2026; arXiv:2610.33956 preprint  
**Canonical source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33956  
**Access level:** arxiv_abstract_and_selected_introduction. The available abstracts and selected sections were inspected, but this is NOT a claim of exhaustive full-text analysis.

## What was studied and how
120 expert-authored tasks with five successive levels (600 levels) across 89 professional roles and 12 occupational domains. Tests staged experience reuse and optional diagnostic feedback/revision across 17 model-harness configurations.

## What the evidence supports
Authors report a median 5.3 percentage-point gain with one feedback-driven revision and substantial remaining difficulty: the strongest configuration clears 47.7% of levels and only 23.3% of all five-level sequences.

## Boundaries and alternative explanations
Human-authored tasks use evaluation agents with rubrics; reliability and domain transfer require separate inspection. Task ladder success differs from individual personal values, actual externally controlled tool effects and clinical safety.

## Architectural inference for Routin (a hypothesis, not a study finding)
Model learning as repeated task episodes, with durable procedures and situation-specific revisions. A user correction is evidence, not automatically a trustworthy causal diagnosis.

## Consequential experiment and negative case
Test five-stage unseen-task ladders with deliberate shifting constraints and withheld tool sets. Compare feedback-only verbal reflection, structured experience and certified procedures, measuring compounding benefit and error propagation.

## What must be checked before a publication-grade claim
Inspect exact version, methods and results, sampling/selection, full tables/figures, statistics, comparison systems and counterexamples. Record stronger conflicting evidence if found. The research only informs the proposed controller; none of these papers independently demonstrates a universal, safe personal secretary or Routin's empirical benefit.
