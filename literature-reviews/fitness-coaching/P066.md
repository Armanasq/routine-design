# P066 — GPTCoach: Towards LLM-Based Physical Activity Coaching

**Authors:** Matthew Jörke, Shardul Sapkota, Lyndsea Warkenthien, Niklas Vainio, Paul Schmiedmayer, Emma Brunskill and James A. Landay  
**Publication:** 2025, ACM CHI 2025, Article 993  
**Primary source:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713819  
**Review evidence:** `acm_open_article_abstract_and_author_pdf_limitations_section`, verified on 2026-11-16. This is an original critical synthesis; methods/results are inspected only to the degree indicated by the access label.

## 1. Question and investigated methods
Formative interviews with 12 health professionals and 10 potential recipients, then a controlled supervised one-session lab study involving 16 participants using three months of historical wearable data; explores motivational interviewing and personalized physical activity coaching.

## 2. Empirical result or theoretical contribution
Participants shared qualitative context and saw perceived potential for individualized coaching; authors explicitly observed difficulties with method adherence, tool calls and appropriate counseling strategies.

## 3. Boundaries, assumptions, negative evidence
Not a longitudinal efficacy study. No evidence the system improved sustained exercise behavior, physical health or safety in unsupervised deployment. Small samples, single-session design, human supervision.

## 4. Concrete Routin policy or architectural implication
Routin can organize optional fitness routines and use valid wearable observations to support reflection, but it must not claim clinical clearance, infer injury recovery or prescribe high-load interventions from weak data.

## 5. Cross-disciplinary mechanism synthesis
Connect JITAI P061 and COM-B P062 for timing and opportunity barriers; the Nature 2026 health agent P059 also distinguishes health-data analysis from coaching/clinical expertise.

## 6. Falsifiable test and acceptance
Compare planning-only vs coaching support with consented user follow-up; track inappropriate advice, sustained user-chosen participation, self-report burden and user overrides. Require professional review for contraindicated contexts.
