# P062 — The behaviour change wheel: A new method for characterising and designing behaviour change interventions

**Authors:** Susan Michie, Maartje M. van Stralen and Robert West  
**Publication:** 2011, Implementation Science 6, 42  
**Primary source:** https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-6-42  
**Review evidence:** `official_open_access_abstract_method_and_model`, verified on 2026-11-16. This is an original critical synthesis; methods/results are inspected only to the degree indicated by the access label.

## 1. Question and investigated methods
Develops and evaluates a synthesis of existing behavior-change frameworks into COM-B (Capability, Opportunity, Motivation -> Behavior) and an intervention-function/policy framework.

## 2. Empirical result or theoretical contribution
Highlights that repeated failure to act can arise from missing practical opportunity or capability as well as motivation. A reminder alone may not address the actual barrier.

## 3. Boundaries, assumptions, negative evidence
COM-B is a theory-informed diagnostic framework, not a device capable of inferring internal motivation from calendar logs. Intervention effectiveness depends on context; the model does not prescribe universal ranking of user goals.

## 4. Concrete Routin policy or architectural implication
On missed study/gym routines, Routin should inspect feasibility, knowledge/support and user-stated motivation as separate explanatory hypotheses. Try adjusting resources/opportunity before treating noncompliance as a user preference.

## 5. Cross-disciplinary mechanism synthesis
Connect to P061 adaptive timing and P065 self-regulation, but avoid measuring hidden mental states without user participation. Preserve personal agency and avoid coercive health/fitness nudges.

## 6. Falsifiable test and acceptance
Use matched missed-routine scenarios with capability, opportunity and declared-motivation causes. Compare generic reminder, feasibility-first analysis and user-validated barrier choice, measuring false attributions, helpful interventions and burden.
