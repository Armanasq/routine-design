# P068 — The Effects of Course-Integrated AI Tutoring on Student Performance and Engagement: A Randomized University Trial

**Authors:** Jing Liu, Tracy Sweet, Marty Haoyuan Chen, Jonathan Engelberg, Megan C. Masters, Martyn Clark, Axel Persaud, Alia Lancaster, Jeffrey K. Hollingsworth and Jennifer King Rice  
**Publication:** 2026, IZA Discussion Paper 18989 / EdWorkingPapers (preprint)  
**Primary source:** https://iza.org/publications/dp/18989/the-effects-of-course-integrated-ai-tutoring-on-student-performance-and-engagement-a-randomized-university-trial  
**Review evidence:** `official_iza_and_edworkingpapers_abstract_and_study_details`, verified on 2026-11-16. This is an original critical synthesis; methods/results are inspected only to the degree indicated by the access label.

## 1. Question and investigated methods
Randomized controlled trial granting access to a course-integrated GenAI tutor across 2,379 undergraduates and 30 instructors at a large U.S. public university; compares course sections and analyzes grades and learning-management-system participation.

## 2. Empirical result or theoretical contribution
Authors report access reduced final grades by 0.37 SD and LMS participation by 0.90 SD relative to controls among same-course sections; larger estimated academic losses for first-generation students. This is an important counterexample to blanket claims that tutor access improves learning.

## 3. Boundaries, assumptions, negative evidence
Working-paper status; full methods and attrition/subgroup analysis must be audited before translating effect sizes. The intervention differs substantially from Kestin et al. (P067) in design, population and engagement patterns; difference is not proof one paper is false.

## 4. Concrete Routin policy or architectural implication
Routin must make *learning outcome* and engagement in relevant study practices primary, not user satisfaction with AI explanations. Tutor behavior should support independent work where mastery is user goal; identify unintended displacement.

## 5. Cross-disciplinary mechanism synthesis
Combine P063 study techniques, P065 self-regulated learning, P067 positive structured tutor and capability-vs-delegation modes. An assistant may be effective for admin tasks while harmful if it replaces practice.

## 6. Falsifiable test and acceptance
Pre-register mastery and engagement guardrails, negative effects by baseline ability, and delayed outcomes. Compare assistant support vs independent practice; stop configurations causing meaningful academic displacement.
