## 1. Research problem and actual methodology
Uses an observation log, retrieval guided by recency/importance/relevance, reflection and planning to simulate interactions among 25 agents in a sandbox town. Component ablations examine perceived behavioral believability.

## 2. Findings supported by the assessed source
Demonstrates a credible interaction architecture for emergent social simulations, but human-judged believability is not verifiable real-world commitment fulfillment. A model that sounds like it knows a person may still act on false inferred memories.

The findings apply to the paper's studied population, benchmark and method. A derived design for Routin is an engineering hypothesis, not an observed effect of the paper.

## 3. Assumptions, caveats and counterexamples
Routin can use episodic evidence and periodic tentative reflection, but promoted semantic memories need provenance, temporal validity and explicit separation of user goals from inferred narrative. Planning should not follow unvalidated personality summaries.

## 4. Consequence for Routin's cognitive architecture
Contrast with MemPABench P041 and MAGE P046: a reflection can be plausible without being behaviorally correct or execution-state coherent. Use evidence-gated promotion rather than reflection-only learning.

## 5. Cross-paper mechanism and what it enables
Evaluate high-conflict histories where repeated events contradict a direct instruction; measure whether reflections cause wrong actions or incorrect long-term preference generalization.
