# P065 — Becoming a Self-Regulated Learner: An Overview

**Authors:** Barry J. Zimmerman  
**Publication:** 2002, Theory Into Practice 41(2), 64–70+  
**Primary source:** https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ656632  
**Review evidence:** `eric_bibliographic_abstract_and_phase_framework_excerpt`, verified on 2026-11-16. This is an original critical synthesis; methods/results are inspected only to the degree indicated by the access label.

## 1. Question and investigated methods
Foundational review of self-regulated learning conceptualizing cyclical phases of forethought (goal/strategy), performance (attention and self-observation) and self-reflection (evaluation and adaptation).

## 2. Empirical result or theoretical contribution
Learning requires active participation and feedback; planning alone does not establish progress. Reflection can change strategies rather than merely the quantity of time allocated.

## 3. Boundaries, assumptions, negative evidence
Conceptual theory rather than a validated plug-and-play AI tutor. The apparent causal role of self-regulation measures can vary by study and course; introspective confidence is not equivalent to externally measured mastery.

## 4. Concrete Routin policy or architectural implication
Routin should support user-chosen learning goals through planning, opportunities to perform independently and periodic evidence-based reflection. It must allow delegation for tasks where learning is not the purpose.

## 5. Cross-disciplinary mechanism synthesis
Combine with P063/P064 active learning and with P061 selective intervention; compare AI tutor studies P067/P068 before assuming supportive tutoring always helps.

## 6. Falsifiable test and acceptance
Test whether the learn-mode follows a plan->practice->assessment->replan cycle without bypassing independent performance or generating excessive check-ins. Evaluate delayed mastery and user effort.
