## What the theory adds
Bratman's planning theory distinguishes future-directed intention from merely wanting or predicting something. Intentions are commitments within partial, hierarchically organized plans that coordinate activity across time and among people. These are normative philosophical claims, not an experimentally fitted psychological model.

## Required semantic distinctions
A user saying they might visit family is not authorizing a booking. A calendar full of late meetings is not evidence of an intention to keep working late. A promise to a colleague is stronger than a tentative internal plan. Routin must type desire, stated intention, proposed obligation, accepted interpersonal commitment and operational grant independently.

## Derived decision logic
Permit local refinement of a partial plan within existing boundaries. Do not infer a new user-end from repeated historical activity. Before moving a social commitment, check what counterpart agreement would be broken. Before sending any external message, check a separate authorization record.

## Relation to Suchman and AGM
P038 prevents treating plans as scripts; P044 provides formal tools for revising conflicting beliefs. Neither grants the system authority to choose the user's values.

## Test
Evaluate intent classification on conditionals, quotes, jokes, reports of other people's plans, corrections and changing commitments; track false acceptance and unauthorized action attempts, not simply extraction F1.
