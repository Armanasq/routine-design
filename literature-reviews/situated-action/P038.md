## Intellectual challenge
Situated action cautions that a plan is not a complete script from which every real-world action follows. Material, organizational and social circumstances change the meaning of an action as it unfolds. The source is a theoretical/ethnographic critique, not evidence that one planning algorithm outperforms another.

## Implication for Routin
Do not represent a week as a rigid sequence of required activities. Represent stable ends and accepted constraints separately from defeasible means, current resource choices and projected steps. Let the planner revise lower-level means while requesting authorization to revise interpersonal commitments or user-chosen ends.

## Failure example
The user declares a learning objective and attempts evening reading twice, unsuccessfully. A mechanical habit learner infers they dislike learning. Situated reasoning tests whether evening conditions or the chosen method were impractical before reconsidering the goal.

## Cross-paper result
Bratman (P039) explains why intentions should remain coherent across time; stable scheduling (P017) explains how to limit needless replanning; adaptive self-regulation (P013) allows a goal to be pursued through changing means.

## Test
Generate contexts with disrupted routines and uncertain arrivals. Compare a fixed plan, unconstrained replanning and intention-preserving local repair. Score accepted-plan churn, user-end preservation and missed essential obligations.
