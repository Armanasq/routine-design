# P056 — The Episodic Buffer: A New Component of Working Memory?

**Authors:** Alan Baddeley

## 1. Research problem and actual methodology
Revises the earlier Baddeley–Hitch working-memory theory with an episodic buffer: a limited-capacity temporary store capable of binding information from different modalities and long-term memory into a coherent episode.

## 2. Findings supported by the assessed source
The buffer is a cognitive theory of human attention and memory, not a token-selection algorithm or empirical bound on an LLM's prompt size. Brain analogies should not be used to claim cognitive equivalence.

The findings apply to the paper's studied population, benchmark and method. A derived design for Routin is an engineering hypothesis, not an observed effect of the paper.

## 3. Assumptions, caveats and counterexamples
Routin's working context should bind necessary current observations, source-backed goal, temporal constraints, interlocutors and relevant episodes into one coherent decision-specific "capsule"; its capacity is measured in tokens and effective downstream accuracy, not a human span value.
