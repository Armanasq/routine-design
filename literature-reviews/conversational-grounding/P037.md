## Main mechanism
Communication is coordinated action requiring shared understanding sufficient for the current purpose. The least-collaborative-effort principle minimizes the total work of contribution and mutual acceptance, not just the speaker's word count.

## New representation
Separate five states: message composed, provider accepted, recipient interpreted, agreement negotiated, and external obligation achieved. Only the last state satisfies the underlying responsibility. Grounding can be implicit for small risks or require explicit confirmation for irreversible commitments.

## Practical implications
A one-line voice instruction may be understood yet not authorized. A meeting invitation may reach the recipient yet remain unaccepted. A terse response that creates three corrective exchanges is worse than a slightly longer initial message. Routin's action policy should include the recipient's expected repair effort and relationship risk where knowable.

## Limits
The chapter is a theory of communication; it does not estimate universal correction costs for AI. Cultural conventions and team norms vary. Never infer consent to consequential actions from silence.

## Integration and experiment
